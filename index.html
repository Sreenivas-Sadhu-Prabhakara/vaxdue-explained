The problem

A wall chart says "6 weeks." It never says which Tuesday.

Every clinic wall chart lists doses by age—at birth, 6 weeks, 10 weeks, 9 months. A tired new parent is left to do the calendar arithmetic in their head: count six weeks off a birthday, remember which months have 30 days, then do it again for the next dose, and the next.

Miss the arithmetic and you miss the window. That is the exact gap vaxdue closes.

How vaxdue solves it

Enter one date. The birch-trunk timeline dates itself, dose by dose.

vaxdue adds each published age-offset to the date of birth with calendar arithmetic that clamps month-ends and handles leap years—so every dose lands on a real calendar date, not a vague age. Watch the trunk grow:

The guarantee

The record can't leave the device — the browser blocks it.

Privacy here isn't a promise in a policy; it's enforced by the page itself. vaxdue ships a Content-Security-Policy with connect-src 'none'. That single line tells the browser to refuse every network request the page could ever make.

No servers, no accounts, no analytics, no fonts phoned home. Your child's name, date of birth, and ticked doses stay in this browser's local storage—full stop.

A short tour

Four things the chart does for you

IAPNIS

IAP and NIS, never blended

Switch between the Indian Academy of Pediatrics schedule (private-practice standard) and the Government NIS (free at public facilities). Two separate corpora—each row carries its own source. Your paediatrician decides which applies.

●due ⚠overdue

Due & overdue at a glance

Every dose is upcoming, due, overdue, or done—shown with a glyph and a word, never colour alone. Overdue and due-now doses surface in a summary strip at the top so nothing hides.

Printable clinic sheet

One button prints a clean A4 sheet—dates, checkboxes, the child's name and date of birth, the schedule edition and disclaimer in the footer—to carry to appointments. CSV export too.

offline

Works 100% offline

No network means it keeps working on the clinic Wi-Fi that never connects. Save up to three child profiles for siblings and twins—each stored only on this device.

Informational reference only — not medical advice. vaxdue and this explainer do not diagnose, treat, or replace a clinician. Your paediatrician's schedule always overrides the chart. Dates are computed from a dated snapshot of the published IAP and Government NIS schedules; schedules are revised over time, so confirm current recommendations at your clinic. Catch-up for missed or delayed doses is out of scope—the chart flags a dose overdue but a doctor must plan the catch-up. The official immunization card issued by your health provider remains the record of truth.
